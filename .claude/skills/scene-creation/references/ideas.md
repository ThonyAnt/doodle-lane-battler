# Scene ideas

A bank of scenes that have been pitched to the owner. Use it for inspiration, or build one of these when asked. Each
follows the same shape as the spreadsheet: a late-90s program open on the desktop, someone using it, and the fight
happening inside the document.

When you pitch new ideas, use this same three-column shape. It forces each idea to answer the two questions that matter:
what changes under the fighters, and why does that make the fight different from every other scene?

| Scene | What happens | Why it plays differently |
|---|---|---|
| **Slideshow** | A presenter clicks through a pitch deck. Each slide is a new layout: bullet points as ledges, a bar chart as stairs, a pie chart that slowly turns. Slide transitions (wipe, blinds, dissolve) sweep across the stage and shove whoever is in the way. | The whole arena is replaced every ten seconds or so, so nobody can camp. |
| **Install wizard** | A progress bar grows across the stage as the floor. Error dialogs pop up as solid blocks. It stalls at 99%, then the machine crashes and the floor drops out. | One clear build-up to a single big moment, with a shrinking safe area before it. |
| **Disk tidy-up** | The stage is a grid of coloured blocks that a tool rearranges one at a time, left to right, carrying fighters with them. A scanning line sweeps across and hurts anyone it touches. | Platforms never stop moving, and the sweep forces both fighters the same way. |
| **Inbox** | Emails drop from the top and stack into ledges. A "reply all" storm sends envelopes flying sideways. Emptying the trash deletes the bottom rows. | The arena fills up and gets cramped, then suddenly opens. |
| **Word processor** | A writer types a letter and each line is a ledge that appears as it is typed. Backspace eats the ledge under you, and spell-check squiggles sting. | Ground appears and vanishes a little at a time, so footing is always uncertain. |

The owner liked all five. The slideshow was recommended as the first to build (most variety for the least new
machinery), the install wizard second (the strongest single moment).

## Seeds for more

Programs from the era that have not been pitched yet, each with the one interface element that would carry the scene:

- **Media player**: the equaliser bars are the floor and move with the music.
- **File manager**: folders open into rooms; drag-and-drop carries fighters between panes.
- **Dial-up connection**: the page loads top to bottom, one strip at a time; the connection drops.
- **Screen saver**: nobody is at the desk; pipes or flying shapes grow through the arena until the mouse moves.
- **Print queue**: pages feed across the stage as moving floors; a paper jam folds them.
- **Calculator or calendar**, **system shutdown** ("It is now safe to turn off your computer" as a final, empty arena).

Avoid building a scene that is a recreation of a specific existing game (a card game win animation, a mine-clearing
grid, a pinball table). The scenes parody kinds of office software, with invented names; they are not copies of
someone else's game.
